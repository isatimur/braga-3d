The old town on foot

Five places in the old town, from the Biscainhos palace to the castle keep, in walking order.

Open this route on the 3D map

  1. Biscainhos Museum

    Stop 1 of 5 · 17th century

    The palace was built in the early 17th century for Constantino Ribeiro do Lago.

    The museum opened on 11 February 1978, presenting aristocratic life from the 17th to 19th centuries through furniture, porcelain, silver and tableware.

    In the real city

    The palace is two minutes from Arco da Porta Nova. Leave time for the garden, especially in spring and summer.

    Sources

    • Palácio dos Biscainhos — Wikipédia (pt)
    • Palácio dos Biscainhos — site oficial

  2. Arco da Porta Nova

    Stop 2 of 5 · 1772

    In 1512, Archbishop Diogo de Sousa opened a new gateway through Braga’s medieval walls, leading to the main shopping street, Rua do Souto.

    Its name, Porta Nova, means “New Gate”.

    Compare its two faces.

    In the real city

    Walk through in both directions to compare the façades.

    Sources

    • Arco da Porta Nova — Wikipédia (pt)
    • Arco da Rua do Souto, também conhecido por Porta Nova — Câmara Municipal de Braga
    • Arco da Porta Nova — We Braga

  3. Braga Cathedral

    Stop 3 of 5 · 11th century

    Its Romanesque design drew on Burgundy and Cluny.

    Construction continued into the 13th century, followed by Gothic and Manueline additions.

    The cathedral remains a working church, with worship in the distinctive Braga rite; the former chapter house contains the Treasury Museum.

    In the real city

    Allow an hour for the separate chapels and Treasury visit.

    Sources

    • Sé de Braga — Wikipédia (pt)
    • Braga Cathedral — Wikipedia (en)
    • se-braga.pt

  4. Santa Bárbara Garden and Old Episcopal Palace

    Stop 4 of 5 · 14th–18th centuries

    The garden borders the palace’s oldest, eastern wing.

    Archbishop Gonçalo Pereira began the palace in 1336; Fernando da Guerra expanded it in 1422–1436, with the tower completed by 1439.

    The garden is much younger: José Cardoso da Silva designed it in 1955.

    In the real city

    Spring and early summer bring the brightest flower beds. Come in the morning for softer light and fewer people.

    Sources

    • Jardim de Santa Bárbara — Wikipédia (pt)
    • Garden of Santa Barbara (Braga) — Wikipedia (en)
    • Episcopal Palace, Braga — Wikipedia (en)
    • Paço Arquiepiscopal Bracarense — Wikipédia (pt)

  5. Braga Castle Keep

    Stop 5 of 5 · 14th century

    Around 1300, King Dinis ordered a castle at Braga’s northern approach.

    The square tower rises about 30 metres over four floors.

    The castle lost its military role in the 18th century and was demolished on 15 November 1905, an event celebrated with bands and a procession.

    The keep and neighbouring church tower survived.

    In the real city

    The tower is easy to see from Terreiro do Castelo, two minutes from the Arcada.
