Two thousand years

Five places and two thousand years, from the Roman baths to Bom Jesus.

Open this route on the 3D map

  1. Roman Baths of Alto da Cividade

    Stop 1 of 5 · 1st–2nd centuries AD

    The Romans founded Bracara Augusta around 16 BC.

    The baths on Cividade hill were built in the early 2nd century alongside the theatre.

    Low brick columns supported the floor, allowing hot air from a furnace to circulate beneath it: the hypocaust system.

    In the real city

    The baths are on a quiet hill ten minutes southwest of the cathedral. A combined ticket includes Fonte do Ídolo; the nearby D. Diogo de Sousa Museum adds context.

  2. Braga Cathedral

    Stop 2 of 5 · 11th century

    The cathedral stands over Roman remains and an early Christian basilica.

    Its Romanesque design drew on Burgundy and Cluny.

    In the real city

    Allow an hour for the separate chapels and Treasury visit. Arco da Porta Nova is a three-minute walk away.

    Sources

    • Sé de Braga — Wikipédia (pt)
    • Braga Cathedral — Wikipedia (en)
    • se-braga.pt

  3. Arco da Porta Nova

    Stop 3 of 5 · 1772

    In 1512, Archbishop Diogo de Sousa opened a new gateway through Braga’s medieval walls, leading to the main shopping street, Rua do Souto.

    The present arch was commissioned in 1772 as the city grew beyond its defences.

    The arch’s lack of doors lives on in the local joke, “Are you from Braga?”

    In the real city

    Walk through in both directions to compare the façades. The cathedral is three minutes away and Biscainhos Museum two minutes away, making this a useful starting point.

    Sources

  4. Raio Palace

    Stop 4 of 5 · 1752–1754

    Wealthy merchant João Duarte de Faria commissioned the palace in 1752–1754.

    Miguel José Raio, Viscount of São Lázaro, bought it in 1853 after making his fortune in Brazil.

    He added the blue tiles and gave the house its lasting name.

    In the real city

    The palace is five minutes south of Praça da República. Fonte do Ídolo is just around the corner.

    Sources

    • Palácio do Raio — Wikipédia (pt)
    • Misericórdia de Braga — CIMMB Palácio do Raio

  5. Bom Jesus do Monte Sanctuary

    Stop 5 of 5 · 1722–1811

    Water flows from the eyes, ears, nose or mouth of the fountain figures.

    The staircase climbs 116 metres.

    Its wooded park welcomes pilgrims and visitors; the staircase was conceived as a spiritual journey as well as an ascent.

    In the real city

    TUB bus 2 runs from Avenida da Liberdade to Bom Jesus. Take the funicular up and walk down the staircase to see the fountains.
